# From quantum metrology to noisy readout depth

A sensor can hold a strong phase signal while its detector record reveals little of it. This project asks how many layers of coherent operations, applied **after sensing**, are needed to make that signal readable when each final detector has a fixed error probability.

The [selected review](ANCHOR.md) supplies the metrological foundation. This page develops the additional mechanism in the project's notation. The [30-milestone map](MILESTONES.md) separates background, M01–M12, from the construction and proof, M13–M30. Exact statements and complete arguments have their authoritative homes in [science/](../science/README.md).

## 1. One phase-sensitive bit

Begin with one qubit in an equal superposition. The signal operation generated by $Z/2$ produces, up to an overall phase,

```math
\lvert\psi_\theta\rangle
=\frac{\lvert0\rangle+e^{i\theta}\lvert1\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
```

An $X$ measurement has two outcomes $Y=+1,-1$. Its ideal probabilities are $(1+Y\cos\theta)/2$. If the reported outcome is independently flipped with probability $p$, define $q=1-2p$. The actual likelihood becomes

```math
P_\theta(Y=y)=\frac{1+yq\cos\theta}{2},
\qquad 0<p<\frac12.
```

The error reduces the fringe contrast from one to $q$. Its effect on precision depends on the slope as well as the contrast. For any binary distribution of the form $P(y)=(1+ym)/2$, direct differentiation gives

```math
F_C=\sum_{y=\pm1}\frac{[\partial_\theta P(y)]^2}{P(y)}
=\frac{(\partial_\theta m)^2}{1-m^2}.
```

Thus this sensor has $F_C=q^2\sin^2\theta/(1-q^2\cos^2\theta)$. At a quadrature, where the cosine crosses zero, it gives $q^2$. With independent product probes, informations add, giving an extensive benchmark proportional to $N$.

The quantum Fisher information describes what an unrestricted measurement could extract from the state. The actual classical information describes the specified apparatus and record. A parameter-independent readout unitary preserves the state's QFI but can change how much reaches imperfect detectors. This distinction is the reason to study the readout circuit separately. See [M01–M04](MILESTONES.md#m01) and the [model](../science/model.md#the-information-being-bounded).

## 2. Why ideal GHZ parity becomes unreadable

A GHZ group of $M$ sensors accumulates a relative phase $M\theta$ under the same additive signal. Ideal local $X$ measurements reveal it through the product of their signs, or parity. The phase therefore oscillates $M$ times faster than the one-sensor fringe, and the ideal local information reaches $M^2$.

One wrong detector sign reverses parity. For independent flips, the average sign of the error on each detector is $q$, so the average product is $q^M$. A known phase offset $\phi$ gives

```math
P_\theta(Y=y\mid\phi)
=\frac{1+yq^M\cos(M\theta+\phi)}2.
```

At quadrature, the information is $M^2q^{2M}$. The factor $M^2$ grows polynomially while $q^{2M}$ decays exponentially at fixed $p>0$. A large supplied GHZ state can therefore be a poor sensor with this raw readout.

This failure concerns one protocol. It does not rule out a better state or decoder. The construction below repairs the protocol; the later converse bounds all allowed protocols. Keeping those two claims distinct is essential. See [M05–M08](MILESTONES.md#m05) and [the raw-parity example](../science/interpretation.md#a-sensing-state-and-a-readable-record-are-different-resources).

## 3. Six sensors in two protected triples

Consider six sensing qubits on a line, initially supplied in a single GHZ state. Divide them into two adjacent blocks of three. In each block, a two-CNOT fanout maps $\lvert x00\rangle$ to $\lvert xxx\rangle$. Reverse that fanout after sensing. It leaves one leader per block and returns the other four qubits to zero:

```math
\frac{\lvert000\rangle\lvert000\rangle
  +e^{i6\theta}\lvert111\rangle\lvert111\rangle}{\sqrt2}
\quad\longmapsto\quad
\frac{\lvert0\rangle\lvert0\rangle
  +e^{i6\theta}\lvert1\rangle\lvert1\rangle}{\sqrt2}
\otimes\lvert0\rangle^{\otimes4},
```

where the tensor factors on the right are ordered as leaders followed by the returned qubits. The phase is still $6\theta$. Compression changes where it is stored; it does not undo the signal already accumulated.

Apply a Hadamard to each leader, then repeat each leader's computational label by the forward fanout. If the ideal leader labels are $z_1,z_2$, the terminal blocks are $z_1z_1z_1$ and $z_2z_2z_2$. Their joint basis probabilities are

```math
P_\theta(z_1,z_2)
=\frac14\left[1+(-1)^{z_1+z_2}\cos(6\theta)\right].
```

These labels are correlated. The independent detector errors in their two blocks remain independent, which is the property needed for decoding. The fanout repeats orthogonal basis labels; it does not clone an arbitrary unknown state.

Majority vote in a triple fails only when at least two detectors are wrong:

```math
\epsilon_3=3p^2(1-p)+p^3.
```

Decode both triples and take their parity. Each logical sign is now wrong with probability $\epsilon_3$, so the parity visibility is $v=(1-2\epsilon_3)^2$. At $p=0.1$, $\epsilon_3=0.028$ and $v=0.891136$. At a calibrated quadrature, the protected parity has $36v^2\simeq28.59$ information, compared with $36(0.8)^{12}\simeq2.47$ for six raw noisy signs. These are two attained statistics at the same sensing-qubit count, not a claim that either maximizes the complete detector record's information.

The readout uses **eight CNOT gates in four disjoint two-qubit layers**, plus local gates, one terminal detector use per qubit, and no extra qubits. Preparing the six-qubit GHZ state is a separate resource. The example instantiates the [general block construction](../science/construction.md), whose basic compression–Hadamard–fanout operation is inherited from [Zhou, Michalakis and Gefen](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#zhou2023). It supplies the mechanism behind [M16–M19](MILESTONES.md#m16).

For a general group, distinguish its sensing size $M$ from its readout block size $b$. A group can span $k=M/b$ blocks even though no readout gate crosses a block boundary. Majority decoding, with a fair tie rule for even $b$, yields

```math
v=(1-2\epsilon_b)^k,
\qquad
\epsilon_b\le\frac12e^{-c_pb},
\qquad
c_p=-\log\!\left(2\sqrt{p(1-p)}\right)>0.
```

The exponential reliability gain concerns $b$; the phase gain concerns $M$. Separating these two sizes is what makes shallow readout useful.

## 4. Sensitivity at every phase

For the decoded parity with visibility $v$, the binary-likelihood calculation gives

```math
F_\phi(\theta)
=M^2\frac{v^2\sin^2(M\theta+\phi)}
{1-v^2\cos^2(M\theta+\phi)}.
```

At finite detector error, a fixed setting has blind phases where the derivative vanishes. Choose $\phi=0$ or $\pi/2$ with equal probability and **retain the setting label**. The joint record has information equal to the average of the two conditional informations. Bounding each denominator above by one gives

```math
F_{\rm two}(\theta)\ge\frac12M^2v^2
```

at every phase. The setting is chosen independently of the unknown phase and requires only a local rotation. No calibrated operating point is needed for this lower bound.

A recorded phase chosen uniformly in $[0,2\pi)$ gives the exact average

```math
F_{\rm uniform}(\theta)
=M^2\left(1-\sqrt{1-v^2}\right).
```

It approaches the full ideal fraction as $v\to1$. Two settings suffice for the scaling theorems; the continuum of ideal local settings supports this stronger asymptotic fraction. If a uniformly random phase label is discarded, the averaged parity likelihood is constant and carries no signal.

Both experiments retain period $2\pi/M$ in the unknown phase. They distinguish nearby phases well while leaving distant identical fringes ambiguous. Uniform local Fisher information therefore does not establish a global estimation-risk guarantee. This is the connection between [M07](MILESTONES.md#m07), [M11](MILESTONES.md#m11) and [M20](MILESTONES.md#m20); see the [full phase calculation](../science/construction.md#4-a-fixed-experiment-with-sensitivity-at-every-phase).

## 5. A ceiling for every probe

To show that the construction's depth order is necessary, a GHZ calculation is insufficient. The upper bound must allow arbitrary input entanglement and an arbitrary allowed readout circuit $V$.

First replace each noisy detector by a hypothetical stronger receiver. With probability $1-\eta$ it receives the qubit intact; with probability $\eta=2p$ it receives an erasure flag. Measuring retained qubits ideally and replacing an erasure by a fair bit reproduces the actual detector. Processing a record cannot increase information, so an upper bound for the stronger receiver also bounds the apparatus.

Next follow a local signal generator through the readout:

```math
h_i=V(Z_i/2)V^\dagger,
\qquad C_i=\text{a support of }h_i.
```

The circuit can spread this operator only through its causal cone. An erasure pattern $E$ sometimes contains the whole support $C_i$. In that case the environment receives the entire operator. Weighting that event by its inverse probability defines

```math
a_i(E)=\frac{\mathbf1_{\{C_i\subseteq E\}}}{\eta^{\lvert C_i\rvert}},
\qquad \mathbb E[a_i]=1.
```

For intuition, these are unbiased but fluctuating environmental representatives of the signal terms. Purifying the channel lets one change its environmental unitary without changing the receiver's state. The chosen change removes the representative $A_E=\sum_i a_i(E)h_i$ from the derivative of that purification. Its residual is $K_E=\sum_i(1-a_i)h_i$.

The rigorous QFI argument then bounds information by $4\lVert\mathbb E[K_E^2]\rVert$. The decisive cancellation is

```math
\mathbb E[(1-a_i)(1-a_j)]
=\eta^{-\lvert C_i\cap C_j\rvert}-1.
```

Disjoint supports contribute zero. The coefficient counts intersection size, not input-state correlations. Since each $h_i$ has norm $1/2$, this yields the [support-intersection bound](../science/model.md#t1-the-support-intersection-converse) for every input state.

This is an outline of an advanced proof, not a replacement for it. The [complete converse](../science/converse.md) verifies the environmental operator, derivative, zero-mean residual, mixed-state extension and every inequality. Its gauge is an explicit specialization of [established subset-noise machinery](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#zhou2021). It is a mathematical choice used to bound QFI, not a parameter-dependent operation available to the experimenter. These steps connect [M09–M10](MILESTONES.md#m09) to [M13–M15](MILESTONES.md#m13) and [M21](MILESTONES.md#m21).

## 6. From blocks and cones to the frontier

The upper and lower bounds now use the same physical scale: how many qubits can cooperate during readout.

For nearest-neighbor gates on a full $D$-dimensional cube, depth $d$ limits a causal support to at most $(2d+1)^D$ sites and the number of supports meeting it to at most $(4d+1)^D$. For all-to-all pairings, the corresponding bounds are $2^d$ and $4^d$. Thus fixed readout depth gives at most $O(N)$ information for every probe at fixed detector error.

Constructively, a block of size $b$ suppresses its decision error exponentially. A GHZ group spanning $k$ blocks has reliable parity when $k\epsilon_b$ stays small. It is therefore useful to group at most order $e^{c_pb}$ blocks together, or all available blocks if fewer remain. Each group supplies information proportional to its squared sensing size, and independent groups add.

With active-qubit count $N'$, the resulting finite lower bound has the scale

```math
F_C\ge C_pN'\min\{N',b e^{c_pb}\},
```

for an explicit positive constant $C_p$. The [grouping proof](../science/frontier.md#a-finite-grouping-lemma) treats the final incomplete group. The [tiling argument](../science/frontier.md#full-cubic-lattices) controls the unfilled boundary of a full grid. These finite details prevent a convenient divisibility assumption from silently becoming a theorem hypothesis.

A cube of side $\ell$ permits compression and fanout in $2D(\ell-1)$ layers, so $b=\ell^D$ grows like $(d+1)^D$ before saturation. Combining this construction with the arbitrary-probe ceiling gives

```math
\log\left(1+\frac{\mathcal F(N,d)}{N}\right)
=\Theta_{p,D}\!\left(
\min\{(d+1)^D,\log(1+N)\}\right).
```

Here $\mathcal F$ is the best worst-phase **local** information permitted by the model. Constants depend on fixed $p,D$. The matched quantity is the order of the logarithm; the constants inside the exponential enhancement are not fixed.

To retain nearly all of one global GHZ group's information, choose $b$ proportional to $\log N$ with a sufficiently large coefficient. Then the accumulated block-error probability vanishes. Filling such a block takes order $(\log N)^{1/D}$ depth on a full grid or order $\log\log N$ with all-to-all pairings. The converse makes both threshold orders necessary.

All-to-all connectivity still has a leading-coefficient gap: necessity gives $d\ge\log_2\log N-O(1)$, while this construction uses $d\le2\log_2\log N+O(1)$. An exact all-to-all frontier at each depth is not established. See [M22–M27](MILESTONES.md#m22) and the [finite frontier](../science/frontier.md).

## 7. Which imperfections the theorem covers

The review's noise discussion motivates asking what information survives an imperfect experiment. The location of the imperfection is crucial here. The detector acts after the coherent readout. The result does not reverse sensing decoherence already suffered by the probe, and its gates are ideal. A fixed pre-readout phase-flip channel can reduce GHZ QFI exponentially before this decoder starts.

The detector need not have symmetric binary output. For a fixed finite output alphabet, write $q_x(y)=P(y\mid x)$. Two constants control different parts of the proof:

```math
\eta=\sum_y\min\{q_0(y),q_1(y)\},
\qquad
B=\sum_y\sqrt{q_0(y)q_1(y)},
\qquad \chi=-\log B.
```

The common overlap $\eta$ allows erasure simulation; the discrimination rate $\chi$ makes repeated outputs reliable. For $0<\eta<1$, independent random complements of each block, undone after decoding, symmetrize the marginal logical error. The same parity and grouping arguments follow. The [detector extension](../science/detectors.md) specifies which labels are retained or marginalized and excludes arbitrary noncommuting POVMs.

Several resource changes alter the question. Pair-correlated errors can preserve total parity despite imperfect individual detectors. Repeating nondestructive detector measurements supplies redundancy through additional rounds. Unbounded qubit density invalidates the quoted geometric cone counts. A sparse or disconnected layout need not support the full-cube construction. These are concrete boundaries with [explicit examples](../science/interpretation.md#assumptions-with-concrete-failure-modes), not consequences covered by the theorem.

Preparation is also separate: supplying a GHZ state across many blocks can be much more costly than the terminal decoder. The readout result identifies an additional resource needed to retrieve an already encoded signal. These distinctions are the synthesis of [M28–M29](MILESTONES.md#m28).

## 8. Putting the argument together

The construction and converse meet because detector reliability improves exponentially with block volume, while circuit causality limits the volume available in a given depth. Independent GHZ groups turn that local balance into a precision frontier. The upper bound's universal quantifier is supplied by the environmental calculation, not by extrapolating the GHZ example.

The project inherits its metrological foundations, purification method and basic global decoder.
